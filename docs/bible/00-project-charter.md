# Living Kingdoms — Project Charter

## One-sentence pitch

Build a kingdom, command workers and soldiers in real time, and defeat an enemy stronghold in a fast, approachable Roblox RTS match.

## Product vision

Create the most approachable and satisfying strategy game on Roblox: easy to understand within minutes, deep enough to reward smart decisions, and playable in meaningful 15–30 minute sessions.

## Player fantasy

The player is the commander of an entire kingdom—not merely one avatar. They direct workers, construct a base, grow an economy, form an army, and make strategic choices that decide the match.

## Design pillars

1. **Responsive command:** Units should reliably do what the player expects.
2. **Easy to learn:** New players understand the core loop within five minutes.
3. **Strategic, not exhausting:** Decisions matter more than extreme clicking speed.
4. **Meaningful short sessions:** A player can complete a satisfying match in 15–30 minutes.
5. **Recoverable setbacks:** Mistakes create problems to solve rather than immediate hopelessness.
6. **Roblox-native accessibility:** Mouse, keyboard, touch, and social play are first-class concerns.

## Core loop

Explore → Gather → Build → Train → Command → Conquer

## Chosen product direction

Living Kingdoms begins as a **match-based RTS**. Persistent kingdoms, guilds, world territory, seasons, and long-term progression are possible future layers, but they are not prerequisites for proving the core game.

## Target audience

- Roblox players interested in tycoons, base building, tower defense, and strategy
- New strategy players who find classic RTS games intimidating
- RTS veterans who want the genre’s satisfying command fantasy in a more accessible format

## North-star question

> Does this make commanding a kingdom more fun?

Features that do not clearly support that question should be postponed or rejected.

## Anti-goals

- No pay-to-win military power
- No mandatory waiting timers
- No giant launch scope
- No five factions before one faction is fun
- No permanent architecture work that does not help ship gameplay
- No feature implementation without acceptance criteria

## Development allocation

Atlas and production tooling should consume roughly 20% of effort. The playable game should consume roughly 80%.
